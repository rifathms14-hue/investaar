Rushta brand guidelines

How to use the Rushta logo, colour and type. Every figure on this page was measured from the artwork in the repo, so a developer, a printer or a clinic can rebuild it without asking.

Brand

What Rushta stands for

The story of the mark comes from the founder. The rest is taken from the product proposal and marked as draft. Confirm it with the client before it appears in anything public.

The mark

Four sheets of paper come together to form a medical cross. Each sheet is one of the four parties: doctors, patients, the pharmacy and the clinic. They meet at a single point, so all four are connected there.

Mission (draft)

Keep every prescription visible from the doctor's signature to the last item collected.

Personality

CarefulPlainExactLocalAccountable

Values

  • Boundaries are part of trust. Each role sees only what it needs.
  • Say what is in and what is out.
  • Patient data stays in the Kingdom.
  • Every action leaves a record.
  • Arabic is a first-class language, not a mirror of English.

Voice rules

Name the state. “Partly collected” beats “In progress”.

Say what is hidden and why.

Write Arabic as Arabic. A native writer drafts it, not a line-by-line translation.

We say

  • Collected
  • Partly collected
  • Out of stock
  • Link expired. Ask the clinic for a new one.

We do not say

  • AI-powered
  • Smart scanning
  • Seamless
  • Reads your handwriting

The product stores handwriting and shows it. It never reads it.

Logo

Three lockups

Choose by the shape of the space. The Arabic always sits above the English. The artwork never mirrors, including in right-to-left layouts. In a right-to-left page the lockup can sit at the right edge, but it keeps its own direction.

Horizontal. The default. Documents, headers, slide covers, the footer of a printed prescription.

Stacked. Square and tall spaces: splash screens, posters, social cards.

Mark. Where the name already appears, or the space is small. App icon, avatar, favicon from 24 px.

Mark

Built from circles and the golden ratio

The mark is one arm turned four times. Every curve is a true circular arc, and every radius is a step on the golden ladder, measured in h, half the thickness of an arm. At a 200 px mark, h is 38.2 px. The mark is exactly four-fold symmetric and its width is φ² times the thickness of an arm.

The circle skeleton. Dashed circles carry the swoosh. Dotted circles are the same centres, grown by the channel gap.

The golden rectangle and its spiral. The squares are the radii the mark uses.

Spacing

Size

How small it can go

Tested at real pixel sizes. Below these widths the Arabic dots fill in, or the pinwheel turns back into a plain cross.

Horizontal

120 px on screen, 32 mm in print.

Stacked

90 px on screen, 24 mm in print.

Mark

24 px on screen, 6 mm in print.

Under 120 px wide the English line gets too small to read. Use the mark alone instead. For a 16 px favicon, draw a simplified cross.

Type

Satoshi and Madani Arabic

The logo already pairs them, so the product uses the same pair. Satoshi Bold stems measure 121 units and Madani Medium strokes measure 116, so at the same size the two scripts carry the same weight. In the logo the English is scaled to the width of the Arabic word and sits slightly smaller, so the Arabic leads.

Partners

Rushta next to a clinic

Clinics are the customer, so their name often leads. When both logos sit together, use the partner lockup. This follows the divider logic in Uber's partner lockup and the 2x spacing in Twitch's.

Partner lockup. The partner logo is as tall as the mark, centred on the same line.

Equal billing

φh either side of a divider that is as tall as the mark and about 3 px thick (h/φ⁵), in Ink. Both logos centred on one line.

Clinic leads

On the printed prescription the clinic's logo leads. Rushta appears once, in the footer, using the horizontal lockup at 120 px (32 mm) or wider, and never larger than the clinic's logo.

Misuse

Twelve things not to do

Each of these breaks a rule above. The artwork was made with specific intent, so keep it intact.

Don't stretch or squash.

Don't recolour the mark.

Don't colour the dots. They match the text.

Don't retype the name in another font.

Don't put English above Arabic.

Don't add shadows or effects.

Don't rotate it.

Don't outline it.

Don't mirror it for right-to-left layouts.

Don't crowd it. Keep the clear space.

Don't use the colour version on Emerald.

Don't crop the mark.

Open

Decisions still open

These need an answer from you or the client.

  1. Which t?Satoshi's default t is a plain cross and can read as an f at small sizes. The alternate t.ss03 has a hooked foot. Pick one for the master and retire the other before launch. I would pick the hooked one.
  2. Emerald on white.The light arms are 2.41 to 1 against white. They are big shapes, so the logo reads, but Emerald must never carry text or small interface parts on a light ground. The Arabic dots are set in the text colour, so nothing small is left in Emerald.
  3. Does the name land in Saudi Arabia?I believe روشتة is best known from Egyptian and Levantine Arabic, and that Saudi patients more often say وصفة. I am not certain. Ask native Saudi speakers before the name goes on a clinic door.
  4. Brand green and status green.Emerald is also the colour interfaces use for success. In Rushta, “Collected” is a success state. If the two share a colour, brand chrome starts to look like good news. Give status colours their own tokens and always pair them with a word or icon.
  5. Madani Arabic has no licence on file.The font family arrived without a licence. Satoshi's licence says logos and wordmarks made with it are allowed and can be registered as trademarks. Madani's terms are unknown. Confirm them before the logo ships or the font goes into the product.
  6. Print values and a favicon.CMYK and Pantone are set at the first print proof. A 16 px favicon needs its own simplified drawing, because the pinwheel turns back into a plain cross at that size.
  7. Which Arabic numerals?Western or Eastern digits in the Arabic interface. Pick one rule and apply it everywhere, including Rx numbers and quantities.
